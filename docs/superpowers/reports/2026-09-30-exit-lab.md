# Exit / holding-model lab – same entries, only the exit varies (2026-09-30)

## Result

- **No exit model was approved.**
  - The selection was frozen before any TEST or HOLDOUT number was computed (sha256 `2fb327bc100fb2e5c0e5c0d778c93df175cf2e86196ee5e1169bca8b23c2ac23`).
  - **0 of 1628** exit × entry combinations passed TRAIN (BH q < 0.10), so nothing went on to VALID, TEST or HOLDOUT.
- **Not one combination is positive in all four periods.**
- LIVE is unchanged.

## Method

- **Entries:** exactly the regime lab's.
  - 17 event families × LONG/SHORT plus the random BASELINE every 8 h.
  - Same entry time and price.
  - Initial stop 4 × ATR15 = 1 R.
  - 2,399,663 entries, 135 symbols, 2025-08 → 2026-09.
- **Exits:** 22 variants on identical entries, simulated in one pass over the 5m path.
  - Stop first within a bar.
  - Trailing and BE stops are set at the bar close and apply from the next bar.
  - Signal and momentum exits fill at the next open.
  - Costs:
    - 0.10 % fees;
    - 0.15 % slippage on every stop exit, including trailing and BE;
    - real funding for the actual holding time.
- **Sanity check:** FIXED equals regime-lab G_WIDE exactly (max difference 5e-6 R).
- **Protocol:** TRAIN → VALID → frozen → TEST → HOLDOUT, with the same approval requirement as the regime lab.
- **Code:** `crypto_trading/entry_research/exit_lab.py`.
- **Tests:** `tests/crypto_trading/entry_research/test_exit_lab.py`:
  - no intrabar look-ahead;
  - stop first;
  - BE and time exits;
  - funding;
  - TEST never affects the selection;
  - checkpoints are neither mixed nor duplicated.

## What the numbers say

1. **Trailing SL, BE, ATR trail and MFE lock are all worse than or equal to FIXED.**
   - They catch more small wins: a higher win rate, e.g. 51 % for TRAIL_A0.5_D0.5.
   - But they give up the large ones, and every trailing exit pays stop slippage.
   - MFE capture is negative for every variant. There is favourable excursion (MFE ~0.9–1.0 R), but price comes back just as often as it continues.
2. **A shorter holding time is "less bad", not good.**
   - TIME15m–1h, SIG_OFF and MOM_OFF end at ≈ −0.05 R in every period.
   - That equals the cost of a round trip (0.10 % fees on a ~1–2 % stop) plus a little slippage.
   - So the price move itself is ≈ 0 over short horizons, and what remains is the cost.
   - Random entries behave the same way, which means the signals add no direction.
   - This is exactly the "loses less" pattern the approval rule rejects.
3. **The best TRAIN combinations fall apart afterwards.**
   - NOTP_TRAIL1.5 on CHANGE_POINT SHORT: +0.15 → −0.11 / −0.11 / −0.27.
   - Short with market OI rising: +0.09 / +0.19 → −0.09 / −0.35.
4. **Per signal type (descriptive):**
   - In TEST, the best exit per type is almost always TIME15m/30m, at −0.03 to −0.07 R.
   - Two exceptions are positive in TEST: CAPITULATION_REV LONG with TIME2h (+0.09) and FUNDING_SQUEEZE SHORT with TIME30m (+0.01).
   - Both were found by picking the best of 22 on TEST (data snooping), with few signals, and neither passed TRAIN.
   - Not usable.

## Conclusion

1. The exit is not the missing piece. On 2.4 M entries over 14 months, no exit model turns the existing entries positive after costs on new data.
2. The picture is consistent with the regime lab and the three earlier studies.
3. The signals carry no measurable directional information beyond the cost barrier.
4. Following the user's rule, no further tuning of small exit parameters is done.
5. **Next step: a genuinely new source of information** that is not derived from the same price and volume history. That choice is the user's.

## Known limits

The same limits as the regime lab apply:
- survivorship;
- Binance data as a proxy for BingX;
- no AI step;
- 5m bars with stop first;
- R stored at 0.001 R resolution, clipped at ±32.7 R.

## Reproduce

- `python -m crypto_trading.entry_research.exit_lab`: checkpoints per symbol in `data/entry_research/exit_lab_chunks/`, resumable.
- The run was started as a separate process through Task Scheduler.
- **Run history:** the first scan was stopped by the memory guard before anything was saved. Then checkpoints were added. Finally, a guard that wrongly stopped on an empty checkpoint (RAYUSDT, a dead contract with a constant price) was fixed. The checkpoints were re-tagged only after verifying that they had been produced by exactly the file version whose scan code is unchanged; a diff shows that only `code_hash()` and the guard line differ.
